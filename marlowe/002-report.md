Not a confession. Not yet. But it’s leaning hard enough in one direction to make the floorboards creak.

## What we know

**Theft:**  
A sealed envelope with **$4,000 cash** disappeared from a locked cabinet in the records office.

**Timing:**  
- Envelope last confirmed in cabinet: **9:50 PM**
- Cabinet discovered open: **about 10:25 PM**
- Exterior employee entrances auto-locked: **10:30 PM**
- No alarm triggered.

**Forced entry:**  
Cabinet lock had fresh damage. Looks like it was pried open with a flat metal object.

**Tool found:**  
A **flat-head screwdriver** was found under a desk about six feet from the cabinet. Partial prints on it, but nobody’s compared them yet. Convenient little snake, that screwdriver.

**Mud print:**  
A muddy boot print near the records office window. Estimated **size 11**. Window was closed and locked from the inside when found.

## The badge trail

Security log says:

- **21:08** — Badge C204 exits.  
  That’s **Johnny Still**.

- **21:31** — Badge A113 exits.  
  That’s **Bradley Chooser**.

- **22:14** — Badge A113 enters.  
  Bradley’s badge comes back.

- **22:18** — Records Office Door opened.

- **22:27** — Records Office Door closed.

- **22:29** — Badge A113 exits.

No other employee badge activity between **10:00 and 10:30 PM**.

## Suspects

### Johnny Still
Claims he left around **9:10 PM**.  
Security says his badge exited at **9:08 PM**.

That mostly lines up. Boot size 9. Not impossible, but nothing points hard at him yet.

### Sarah Bowler
Claims she was home all evening.  
No badge activity recorded for her in the relevant window.

Could still be lying, sure. People do that for sport. But right now there’s no evidence placing her in the building.

### Bradley Chooser
Facilities tech. Boot size **11**.  
Claims he left around **9:30 PM**.

Security says his badge exited at **9:31 PM**, which supports the first half of his story.

Then it says his badge came back at **10:14 PM**, entered the records office at **10:18 PM**, left it at **10:27 PM**, and exited the building at **10:29 PM**.

That second half? He forgot to mention it. Funny how memory works when there’s four grand missing.

## My read

**Primary suspect: Bradley Chooser.**

Facts against him:

- His badge returned during the theft window.
- His badge accessed the records office minutes before the cabinet was discovered open.
- His badge exited at **10:29 PM**, one minute before exterior entrances locked.
- His boot size matches the muddy print estimate: **size 11**.
- His job in facilities could plausibly put a screwdriver in his hands, though that’s suspicion, not proof.
- His statement is incomplete or false: he claimed he left around **9:30 PM**, but his badge shows activity after **10:00 PM**.

## The part that bothers me

The muddy boot print was near the window, but the window was locked from the inside. That could mean:

- Someone walked near the window but didn’t use it.
- Someone staged a fake entry/exit point.
- The print is unrelated.

Right now, it’s not enough to hang the case on. But paired with Bradley’s boot size and badge activity, it smells bad.

## Next moves

If this were my case, I’d do three things before calling it closed:

1. **Compare the fingerprints on the screwdriver** against Bradley, Johnny, and Sarah.
2. **Interview Bradley hard** about the 10:14 PM re-entry and 10:18 PM records office access.
3. **Check whether badge A113 could have been borrowed or stolen.**  
   Because if Bradley says someone used his badge, now we’ve got a different kind of lie to test.

## Bottom line

Bradley Chooser is the strongest suspect by a mile.

But the clean case isn’t quite clean yet. Badge, boot print, opportunity, false statement — that’s a nice little pile. Still needs fingerprint comparison or recovery of the money if we want more than a shadow on the wall.
